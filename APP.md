## How the world is made, and growing or moving it

The whole City of Ann Arbor inside its official limits (75.6 km², x -4222..5901, z -4889..6330) is built offline, in
this order, all with `.lamp/reference/venv/bin/python -I <tool> .` from the app folder:

1. `Assets/Tools/fetch-annarbor.py`: the city limits (`.lamp/reference/aa/limits/aa_city_boundary.geojson`, the city's
   "City Boundary" open data), then everything asked for by area over the limits plus about 600 m (its `BB`):
   OpenStreetMap through Overpass (streets, buildings, water, woods, parks, land use, parking, rail, golf, trees; shops
   and amenities; signals and signs; level crossings; roof shapes; fuel; bookcases) and the city's tree canopy. Each
   file remembers its box and is fetched again only when the box changes. The city's other layers (buildings, street
   trees, hydrants, land use, park amenities, streetlights, zoning, historic districts, addresses, parcels, speed
   limits, paving) are whole-city downloads in `.lamp/reference/aa/` already.
2. `Assets/Tools/fetch-3dep.py`: the USGS 1 m bare earth, in 1 km squares over `CX0..CZ1` (now -6000..7000 both ways).
3. `Assets/Tools/build-annarbor.py`: the map (`Web/js/city-geodata.js`, buildings, trees, river, 10 m elevation, curb
   cuts, woods, yards). The detailed area `LAND` is the limits with the township islands filled, buffered 120 m (now x -4677..6041, z -5009..7200, 90 km², with the airport, the freeway loops and the streets in `WHOLE_STREETS`, Elizabeth Road west of Maple, taken in whole with 90 m each side, their houses from OpenStreetMap where the city has no footprints, and their trees from `Assets/Tools/fetch-canopy-extra.py`, which reads tree cover off one small USGS NAIP aerial photo per street into `.lamp/reference/aa/canopy-extra/` since the city's canopy stops at the limits; run it before this step when adding a street):
   everything is clipped to it, and `GEO.edge` is its outline (`TOWN_EDGE`, `inTown` in city-config.js, which keeps
   cars and teleports inside). `GEO.limits` (the real limits and islands) is drawn dashed on the map. `CORE` (the
   first map's box, downtown, campus, Kerrytown, the Old West Side, the Arb and the big parks) is in `GEO.core` and
   `inCore` for anything that wants to spend less outside it (nothing does yet). It ends by running
   `split-geodata.py`, which moves the heaviest layers into `Web/data/` squares streamed by distance.
4. `Assets/Tools/build-ground.py`: the fine ground (`ground.bin.gz`, a 6 m grid of 2049², corner `GX0, GZ0`
   -5304, -5424, 12.3 km a side), the 10 m heights remade from it, and the river's levels.
5. `.lamp/scripts/roof_hills.py . --fix` (roofs the survey kept as ground; copies before it in
   `.lamp/reference/*-before-roofs.bin.gz`), then the smaller builders: build-addresses, build-districts, build-neighborhoods,
   build-street-furniture, build-theride, build-rail-crossings, build-roof-shapes, build-gas-stations.
   And `Assets/Tools/build-shop-types.py` (python3, no venv needed) whenever build-annarbor.py changed the signs: the
   kind of business of each of `GEO.signs`, by name and nearest place, to `Web/data/shop-types.json` in the signs' order.

Heights are metres above `BASE` 227.8 m (pinned so the river's, dams' and landmarks' heights in the code stay put);
the east end down the river lies lower, so heights go down to `LO` -30 m (the ELV2 header's last number; river levels
are signed). Outside `LAND` the outskirts (outskirts.js) are a plain 50 m grid of the real heights with fields and
woods, out 6 km.

To add more area: widen `CX0..CZ1` in fetch-3dep.py and fetch, change the area `LAND` is made from in
build-annarbor.py (the limits, or a polygon of your own) and the fetch box in fetch-annarbor.py,
build-street-furniture.py and build-theride.py, and move `GX0, GZ0` (and `STEP`/`N`, keeping N at 2049 for the
terrain mesh) in build-ground.py so its grid covers it; then run the steps above. The ground's squares and the
geodata's split follow the area by themselves. To move to another place altogether, the world frame (`LAT0, LON0` in
build-annarbor.py) moves with it, and every Ann Arbor source (the city's open data URLs and field names, the USGS
squares, the GTFS feeds for TheRide and the U-M buses, the weather station and river gauge) needs its local
equivalent; the hand-placed things (landmarks and their models, teleports in city-config.js and index.html, the
dams, the cascades, the stadium, camera spots, area labels on the map) are Ann Arbor's and are written in world
metres, so they'd all be replaced.

## Data

Everything ships with the game; the only things fetched while it runs are the live weather (`weather.js`: the National
Weather Service's latest observation at KARB, api.weather.gov, every 15 minutes by the clock, so at once after the page was hidden or asleep; a fair day offline; no personal
details sent) and the Huron's flow (`river-gauge.js`: the USGS gauge "Huron River at Ann Arbor", site 04174500, from the USGS Water Data API (api.waterdata.usgs.gov, latest-continuous),
discharge in cubic feet a second, asked for with the weather; public domain; the kayak's current is scaled by
(cfs/500)^0.4 within 0.45 to 2.4, and runs at 1 offline). All the rest is generated by
`Assets/Tools/build-annarbor.py` (run with the venv's Python in `.lamp/reference/venv`; its downloads are cached in
`.lamp/reference/aa/`), which writes the files below in the same shapes Mr.doob's New York tools did:

- `Web/js/city-geodata.js` (Ann Arbor's map; `GEO.edge` the detailed area's outline): streets, blocks, parks, water and the land's outline from
  OpenStreetMap through the Overpass API (ODbL). World frame: 1 unit = 1 m, x east, z south, true north up
  (`GEO.frame.bearingDeg` 0), origin at Main St & Liberty St (42.27965, -83.74838).
- `Web/js/building-geodata.bin.gz`: the City of Ann Arbor's building footprints (AAGIS, from 2009 LiDAR, with average
  heights; https://ckan.a2gov.org/dataset/building-footprints), OSM heights where it has them.
- `Web/js/tree-geodata.bin.gz`: the City of Ann Arbor's street tree inventory. It states no license: fine to use here,
  but ask the city before publishing or sharing the game widely.
- `Web/data/elevation.bin.gz`, `ground.bin.gz` and `river.bin.gz`: made at build time only by
  `Assets/Tools/build-ground.py` from the USGS 3DEP 1 m bare-earth model (fetched by `Assets/Tools/fetch-3dep.py`,
  public domain), with the open Terrain Tiles beyond its cover; heights relative to the lowest ground in the box
  (downtown is about 28 m up, the river lowest). Run `build-ground.py <app folder>` again after changing the streets. Where the 1 m model kept a big roof as ground (Palmer's parking deck and plaza by Washtenaw, the hospital helipad deck, two Geddes-area roofs), `.lamp/scripts/roof_hills.py` (flags and fills building footprints) and `.lamp/scripts/level_box.py` (a smooth fill of a box from its edges) leveled it after the build; the copies from before are in `.lamp/reference/*-before-roofs.bin.gz`. Run them again after rerunning build-ground.py.

Houses (survey kind 1 under 14 m, and small sheds and garages) are drawn by `houses.js`, not the facade shaders. Each
footprint is split (rasterised at 0.5 m in its oriented box) into a main block, the largest rectangle, and up to three
wings; the main block's roof is OSM's `roof:shape` where mapped (`data/roof-shapes.json`), else chosen from shape and

## Easy to change

- Time of day and the sky's colors: `TIME_PRESETS` in `lighting.js`.
- How a building, the roads or the park look: `tsl-materials.js`, `buildings.js`, `building-details.js`.
- The vehicles: `cars.js`, `bus.js`, `helicopter.js`; how they drive, fly and how fast: `controllers.js`.
- How busy the streets are: the counts in `traffic.js` and `pedestrian-system.js`; by the hour, weekday and term, `town-clock.js`.
- The town keeps Ann Arbor's clock (`town-clock.js`, read by main.js `crowdBusy` for the hour shown: the real Detroit time under Now, each look's own hour otherwise, LOOK_HOUR, with today's weekday). People: campus (State Street, central, North and medical campus, the athletic campus), downtown (with Kerrytown) and the homes each follow their own by-hour table, scaling each sidewalk's place weight (pedestrian-system.js `edgeCrowd`, `setBusy(weather, shares)`; the weather alone is the overall factor). On class days the campus walks fill 1.4 times from :50 to :00 and :20 to :30 (Tower Time: classes start on the hour or half hour and let out ten minutes early); weekends thin campus, and outside term (TERMS / RECESS, Detroit dates: Fall 2026 from Aug 31 and Thanksgiving recess Nov 25-27 per the Regents; the other dates follow the usual pattern and want checking against the Registrar's calendar each year) campus is at a third. Friday and Saturday nights downtown stays full till Michigan's 2 a.m. last call, with a rush 1:50-2:30 and then near-empty streets. Cars: `trafficShare` by hour (weekday rushes; later, flatter weekends; about 15% of the fleet at 3 a.m.), traffic.js `setTrafficShare`: the cars out are the first so many of a fixed shuffled order (`rank`, the cruisers and the ambulance first, so the kinds keep their shares), and one past the count is taken off once out of sight, none brought in for it (`[traffic] N of M cars out for the hour`). ## Golf

Playing the courses (js/golf-play.js, class GolfPlay made in main.js): on foot within 9 m of a tee, with no car, bike or boat in reach to get into, get-in says Play golf. The player is held at the ball (`p.golf`, a pose controllers.js gives `PersonRig.perch`, whose `hands` put both hands on the grip; pedestrians.js) with a club drawn from shapes; the camera goes down the line and then after the ball (cameras.js `override`). Aim is the turn keys or stick, W/S (unlisted) sets how far; the club is picked from the distance and lie and never named. The mouse is a slingshot from where it's pressed: press anywhere on the view and pull back, and the shot goes the other way (the pull read on the ground at the ball's height, from the press's spot), longer the farther the pull on the screen (0.22 of the view's height is full), with the camera held still while pulling; under 8 px of pull it's only a click, and letting go then hits nothing. In split screen it reads player one's half. Holding swing or pulling shows a solid white ring painted flat on the ground (one smooth 256-step band whose edges fade out through a `fade` vertex attribute, laid on the turf so it follows the slopes) at the aimed spot (no dotted line), pulsing between small and large (size from distance and lie); letting go hits to a random spot inside it, and the ring hides once let go. The ball is shown as a lit white golf ball (instanced: the plain ball mesh didn't draw), its true size up close and at most 3x far off, sitting on the grass. The circle's size is frozen at the moment of release (`frozenR`). The golfer's spot is set once per shot as they line up; aiming only turns them. While lining up, the camera sits 9 m behind and 4.5 m over the ball (putting: about 4 m behind), looking at a spot 6 m ahead, so the golfer stands just below the middle of the screen, clear of the golf words. The hole is marked over the view wherever it is, trees in the way or not: a white pointer with its yards on the pin (`tagPin`, an HTML tag placed from the pin's spot on screen). Ball: flight without drag; on its first landing what's left of its speed is set to carry it the club's real roll-out on a fairway (`run` in CLUBS: the driver 24 m down to the wedge's 2.5 m, less for a shorter swing) times the surface's share (green 0.7, fairway and tee 1, rough 0.2, bunker none), hops and roll together; then bounce and roll by surface (golf.js layOutHoles' greens, bunkers, tees, fairways; rough elsewhere), down the ground's slope, holed under 1.7 m/s at the cup; water (the river) and out of bounds (85 m off the hole's line in the rough) are a stroke and the shot again. Holes: Huron Hills' 18 from golf-holes.js in order; every other course's numbered holes from golf-courses.js (Assets/Tools/build-golf-courses.py: OSM golf=hole ways from .lamp/reference/aa/osm.json, given to the course outline holding each; tee and pin snapped to the nearest mapped tee box and green). A round goes by number on its course, round to 1 after the last, and ends ("Round over", the golfer let go) once all are played. `COURSE.v` rebuilds the cached list when it changes. While golfing the player is held on their planted spot: a nudge under 6 m (a car brushing them) puts them back; golf cart paths (golf=cartpath/path) are left out of the streets in build-annarbor.py, so no cars drive or park on them; farther (a teleport) ends the round. Each course is a landmark (city-config.js, art 'golf', at its first tee; Huron Hills' Teleport row reads E Huron River Dr, its address): named when you're there, flown round with the view key, its flag pinned on both maps, and a Golf section in the Teleport menu lands you on foot behind tee 1 (GOLF_TEES); courses mostly past the town's playable edge (Barton Hills, Lake Forest, Stonebridge) are left out by build-golf-courses.py. Both maps (city-map.js) draw that landmark as a golf flag sign (green disc, white stick, yellow flag) at each course's hole-1 tee, at every zoom. Huron Hills' pars are its published scorecard's (golf-holes.js, par 67: 4 5 3 3 4 3 5 4 4 / 4 3 4 4 3 4 3 3 4, checked against Hole19's card); Leslie Park's 18 (par 72) and the U-M course's come from the map's own hole tags; elsewhere par from length when the map has none. The round's last hole says so in its hole notice ("· last hole"); a full round's end is set against the best one on that course (`golfBest`, saved: "Round over · 71 for 18 holes (+4) · best 69", or "· a new best"). A round in progress is saved (`golfRound`, its snapshot) at each tee and each stop of the ball and cleared when it ends or is stopped; on a fresh start (main.js init, when no live-change snapshot is pending) `resume` takes it up, the player (on foot) teleported back to its ball (`teleportActiveTo` first, so the jump isn't taken for a push) ("Round goes on · Hole 7 · …"). Sounds are made in code (club: face click, inharmonic metallic ring, crack and air; putt: soft low tock; cup) and follow the sound button. The round is in main.js's snapshot (`golf`), its hole and the holes played kept by course and number, so a change to the course data doesn't move it.

Every hole's tee has two blue markers and a post with a green board facing the way players come up (the hole's number, its par and its yards, painted into one canvas sheet, one draw; hidden well away from the course, scene-culling.js; `TEE_MARKS_V` rebuilds them in the open game). Golf things sit on the drawn turf, about 0.2 m over the ground's height (`turf`). No tracer: the ball is drawn bigger with distance so it can be followed. A Chip club (32 m) is picked inside 34 m; a hole is picked up at five over par. The golf words sit above the control hints.
